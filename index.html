01 · reference

Overview

Not a lecture — just the rules and terms that actually get tested.

LAW 01

The ring is the electron source

The π-cloud above and below the ring plane makes benzene a base. It reacts with electron-poor reagents — electrophiles or acids.

LAW 02

Substitution, not addition

Alkenes undergo electrophilic addition; aromatic rings undergo electrophilic substitution, because substitution keeps the resonance stabilization intact.

LAW 03

Reaction families

Nitration, sulfonation, halogenation, and Friedel-Crafts reactions occur for nearly all aromatic rings.

nitrosationdiazo couplinghighly reactive rings only
LAW 04

Activating vs. deactivating

A group is activating if it makes the ring more reactive than benzene, deactivating if it makes it less reactive.

LAW 05

ortho,para vs. meta directors

A group is an ortho,para director if attack occurs mainly next to and across from it, a meta director if attack occurs mainly meta to it.

LAW 06

Measuring reactivity

Compare (a) time to react under identical conditions, (b) severity of conditions needed, or (c) rate in a competitive reaction against benzene.

LAW 07

Directive power ranking

−NH₂, −OH > −OCH₃, −NHCOCH₃ > −C₆H₅, −CH₃ > meta directors

LAW 08

Halogens are the exception

F, Cl, Br, and I are deactivating yet still ortho,para-directing — the one class that breaks the usual pairing.

LAW 09

Two groups on a ring

Reinforcing groups predict cleanly. Opposing groups need a large gap in directive power to win clearly, and little substitution happens between two groups that are meta to each other.

LAW 10

Order matters in synthesis

The sequence you introduce substituents — and when you convert one group into another — decides which isomer you isolate.
